# Process

## Initial Ideas

I began with three ideas:

1. AI may make writing code less necessary, but reading and evaluating code will remain important.
2. Designers do not need to become professional programmers, but they should understand computational systems.
3. AI makes solutions easier to obtain, but receiving a solution is not necessarily the same as understanding it.

I used ChatGPT to help organize these ideas into an essay structure. I did not begin by asking it to generate a complete essay. My goal was to first define my own position and then use AI to test and organize the argument.

## Developing the Argument

I decided to use my experience learning technical art and Unreal Engine as the main concrete example. It reflects the difference between receiving instructions and understanding a system.

One of my original ideas was that AI can make beginners lazy or overly dependent on easy answers. I decided not to use that wording because it felt too broad and judgmental. ChatGPT helped me reframe the idea as a distinction between "getting a solution" and "developing understanding." I kept this version because it describes the problem more precisely and also matches my own experience.

I also decided not to argue that AI cannot replace human creativity. I do not fully agree with that claim, because AI is already capable of performing useful creative and technical work. My argument is instead about the knowledge designers need in order to remain able to judge and control that work.

## Final Revision

In the final revision, I narrowed the essay from a general discussion of whether AI will replace programmers into a more specific argument about how programming literacy is changing for designers.

ChatGPT suggested several broader directions, including arguments about human creativity and historical comparisons between AI and earlier technologies. I decided not to make these the main focus because they moved the essay away from my own experience.

The idea I kept was the distinction between writing code and understanding computational systems. I also kept the idea that "a solution is not the same as understanding" because it closely reflects my experience learning Unreal Engine and technical art.

AI was used for brainstorming, organizing the essay structure, testing alternative arguments, and editing English. I used my own experience with illustration, technical art, and Unreal Engine as the main evidence for the final position.
